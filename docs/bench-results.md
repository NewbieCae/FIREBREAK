# Firebreak benchmark — results

> Generated by `uv run python scripts/bench.py --seeds 500 --p 0,0.1,0.2,0.3`. Do not edit by hand.

500 synthetic worlds per value of p (5 companies, 300 operations each, generator `src/firebreak/synth.py`). In each world, a company drawn at random from **all** of them (from the upstream supplier `e0` to the final customer `e4`) is compromised from a time `since` onwards. **Ground truth**: its records issued after `since` and all their descendants through the *true* dependencies, except `verified` records. The engine only sees *recorded* dependencies: each true dependency (a record's input or a send between companies) is hidden with probability **p**. A hidden send leaves a reception with no token or link. In addition, 10% of receptions come from outside the federation: they are legitimate, also have no token, and are therefore indistinguishable in the ledgers.

Compromised companies drawn (at p = 0): `e0` (106), `e1` (111), `e2` (92), `e3` (96), `e4` (95).

No company is excluded from the draw.

Policies, applied to the same world:

- **A — Alert only**: everyone is warned, nothing is blocked.
- **B — Block supplier + partners**: every record dated `since` or later at the compromised company and its direct customers is blocked (the "cut off the supplier" response).
- **C — Firebreak**: the repository's engine (`Simulation.declare_compromise`), propagation over the recorded graph + signed alerts.
- **D — Firebreak + caution** (benchmark only): C, plus quarantine of receptions **with no recorded provenance** (no token or link) dated `since` or later at the known direct partners of the compromised company, and of everything that depends on them in the recorded graph. D uses the "missing provenance" signal, read only from engine state: never the ground truth nor the "hidden dependency" flag.

Values: mean ± 95% CI half-width (normal approximation). "n/a": undefined (A blocks nothing, so it has no precision).

## Reading the results

- A world has 300 records on average, of which 30.4 ± 1.0 are actually contaminated, including 11.1 irreversible actions (payment, shipment, production launch).
- p = 0: Firebreak blocks 4.5 times fewer healthy operations than partner blocking (9.6 vs 43.2), for a recall of 100.0% vs 98.5% and 0.00 leaked irreversible action(s) vs 0.07.
- p = 0.1: Firebreak blocks 4.9 times fewer healthy operations than partner blocking (8.8 vs 43.2), for a recall of 98.0% vs 98.5% and 0.15 leaked irreversible action(s) vs 0.07.
- p = 0.2: Firebreak blocks 5.1 times fewer healthy operations than partner blocking (8.4 vs 43.2), for a recall of 95.7% vs 98.5% and 0.32 leaked irreversible action(s) vs 0.07.
- p = 0.3: Firebreak blocks 5.4 times fewer healthy operations than partner blocking (7.9 vs 43.2), for a recall of 93.4% vs 98.5% and 0.45 leaked irreversible action(s) vs 0.07.
- **A** lets all contamination through (recall 0), including 11.1 irreversible actions per world on average: that is the cost of "warning without acting".
- **B** does not depend on p (it uses no recorded dependency). It stops almost all contamination in these worlds, at the cost of blocking a large share of healthy activity.

## Limitations

- **Cautious origin rule.** At p = 0, Firebreak blocks 9.6 healthy operations per world, 9.6 of them (100.0%) because of the origin rule: any data attributed to the compromised company after `since` is quarantined, even if it is a copy of earlier or verified data, along with whatever depends on it. This is a deliberately cautious engine choice, not a propagation error.
- **Missing dependencies.** Firebreak only sees what is recorded. C's recall drops from 100.0% at p = 0 to 93.4% at p = 0.3 (5th percentile 79.2%, worst case 66.0%); at p = 0.3, 28.4% of worlds let at least one contaminated irreversible action through.
- **D uses the "missing provenance" signal.** At p = 0.3, D gains +4.4 recall points over C for +5.1 healthy operations blocked per world; at p = 0, where nothing is hidden, D only costs the noise (+2.1) of legitimate out-of-federation receptions. Its gain rests on a simulator assumption: a missing send dependency always leaves a visible trace (an unattributed reception). A missing internal dependency leaves no trace, and D does not catch it. D's figures are therefore an **upper bound** for this signal.
- Everything is simulated: the figures describe our supply chain model, not a real company.

### Sensitivity: limitations and conclusions

- **Inter-company relationships get denser with size** (unchanged generator): 1.16 direct customer(s) per company at 5, 1.67 direct customer(s) per company at 8, 2.29 direct customer(s) per company at 12. Partner blocking (B) therefore hits more companies as the chain grows; share of operations actually contaminated: 10.1% at 5, 5.9% at 8, 4.1% at 12.
- **Collateral B / C**: 4.5× at 5 cos., p = 0, 5.4× at 5 cos., p = 0.3, 5.5× at 8 cos., p = 0, 6.7× at 8 cos., p = 0.3, 7.1× at 12 cos., p = 0, 9.0× at 12 cos., p = 0.3.
- **Missing dependencies by size** (p = 0.3): C recall 93.4% at 5, 93.8% at 8, 92.2% at 12; B recall 98.5% at 5, 98.4% at 8, 98.3% at 12; worlds where C lets an irreversible action through 28.4% at 5, 22.6% at 8, 27.8% at 12.
- **Cost of the "missing provenance" signal** (p = 0.3): at 0% noise, D catches 1.9 leak(s) for +3.6 healthy blocked, i.e. 1.8 healthy blocked per leak caught; at 10% noise, D catches 1.6 leak(s) for +5.1 healthy blocked, i.e. 3.1 healthy blocked per leak caught; at 30% noise, D catches 1.1 leak(s) for +7.5 healthy blocked, i.e. 7.0 healthy blocked per leak caught.
- At 0% noise and p = 0, D blocks +0.0 more healthy operation(s) than C: with no out-of-federation reception, the signal has no false positives, which never happens in a real supply chain. D remains an upper bound.

Conclusions from `docs/fiche-defense.md` put to the test:

- ✅ unchanged: Firebreak blocks fewer healthy operations than partner blocking, at every size and every p.
- ✅ unchanged: Firebreak's recall is 100% at p = 0 at every size.
- ✅ unchanged: at p = 0.3, Firebreak's mean recall stays below partner blocking's (accepted trade-off).
- ✅ unchanged: D catches leaks missed by C at p = 0.3 whatever the noise.
